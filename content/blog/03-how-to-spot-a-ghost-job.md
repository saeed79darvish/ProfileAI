# How to Spot a Ghost Job Before You Apply

A ghost job is a listing that stays posted after a company has stopped actively hiring for it — sometimes because the role got filled quietly, sometimes because it never had real budget behind it, sometimes because a company keeps postings live to build a resume pipeline or project growth to investors. A 2024 Clarify Capital survey found roughly 1 in 5 employers admitted to keeping fake or stale listings posted. You can't always tell from a single posting, but a handful of concrete signals make it far more likely.

## TL;DR

- A posting open more than 30-45 days with no repost or edit is a warning sign, not proof.
- "Always hiring" or evergreen listings from staffing agencies and large companies are frequently ghost postings by design.
- No hiring manager name, no specific team, and a vague salary range are correlated with lower response rates.
- Cross-check the listing date across multiple job boards — reposting the same listing repeatedly is a red flag.
- You can't fully avoid ghost jobs, but you can stop wasting hours tailoring applications to them.

## Why Companies Post Jobs They're Not Actively Filling

This isn't always malicious. A few real reasons ghost postings exist:

- **Pipeline building.** Some companies keep a posting live year-round for high-turnover or hard-to-fill roles (sales, nursing, some engineering specialties) so they always have a stack of candidates ready.
- **Internal politics.** A posting goes up to satisfy a headcount approval process, then the role gets frozen or reassigned internally, but nobody takes the listing down.
- **Signaling.** Some companies keep job postings visible to appear like they're growing, whether to investors, competitors, or their own employees.
- **Passive sourcing.** Recruiters sometimes keep evergreen listings open specifically to build a resume database for future roles that don't exist yet.
- **Genuine lag.** Sometimes it really is just administrative slowness — the role got filled internally or the req got paused, and nobody updated the board. This one isn't a ghost job in the deceptive sense, just an operational gap.

## The Signals Worth Checking

None of these alone proves a posting is dead. Together, they shift the odds a lot.

| Signal | What to check | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Posting age | Look for a "posted X days ago" tag | Postings open 30+ days with no edits are statistically less likely to be actively screened |
| Repost pattern | Search the exact job title + company on multiple boards | Companies that repost the same req every few weeks are often just refreshing the date, not restarting a search |
| Specificity | Read the description closely | Vague team names, no manager mentioned, generic "growing team" language correlates with lower-intent postings |
| Application volume shown | LinkedIn shows "over 200 applicants" | High volume alone doesn't mean it's fake, but combined with posting age it's a useful signal |
| Salary transparency | Is there a real range, or a huge spread ($60K-$150K)? | An unrealistically wide range often signals the role isn't well-defined yet, or isn't a real near-term hire |
| Company hiring freeze news | Check recent layoff or hiring-freeze reporting on the company | Postings left up during or right after a public freeze are more likely stale |

## What to Do When You Suspect a Ghost Job

You don't have to avoid these postings entirely — sometimes a role really is still live even at 60 days old — but you should change how you spend your time on them:

1. **Don't over-tailor.** Save the full tailoring effort (see our [resume tailoring guide](/blog/how-to-tailor-a-resume-to-a-job-description)) for postings that show stronger signals of being active.
2. **Apply, then move on.** Submit a solid but not maximally customized application and don't wait to hear back before applying elsewhere.
3. **Try to route around the board.** If you have any path to a referral or a direct message to someone on the team, that beats the application queue on a stale listing regardless of whether it's a true ghost job.
4. **Track your source, not just your outcome.** If you notice a specific company or board consistently produces zero responses across multiple applications, that's a pattern worth weighting into future decisions.

This is also why Profile AI's job feed applies a ghost-job likelihood score to postings before they ever reach your queue — postings that show strong stale-listing signals get demoted in your feed rather than deleted, so you can still see them if you want but you're not spending your best tailoring effort on a req that's already dead.

## FAQ

**Is it illegal for companies to post fake job listings?**
No, in most jurisdictions there's no law against it, though some U.S. states and the EU have proposed transparency requirements around active hiring status. It's a gray area, not a crime.

**How common are ghost jobs really?**
Survey estimates vary widely — from roughly 1 in 5 employers admitting to it in a Clarify Capital survey to higher estimates from other sources. There's no single authoritative number because companies rarely disclose it directly; the honest answer is "common enough to change your strategy, not common enough to assume every posting is fake."

**Should I stop applying to postings that look like ghost jobs?**
Not necessarily — apply, but budget your time accordingly. A five-minute application to a possible ghost job is a reasonable bet. A ninety-minute deeply tailored application to the same posting is a worse use of your time than spreading that effort across postings with stronger active-hiring signals.

**Does reapplying to the same ghost job later ever work?**
Occasionally, yes — if the role genuinely reopens with a new req, a prior application can actually help since you're already in the system. It's not a reliable strategy to count on, though.
